# Sealed-bid auction lifecycle

Each `commit_bid(round_id, participant, commitment)` call requires the participant's authorization, an eligible member with a request in the round, `Commit` status, and ledger time strictly before the commit deadline. Only one 32-byte commitment is stored per `(round_id, participant)`. The commitment is the unchanged [Step 1 hash](step1-integration.md); neither bid amount nor secret is passed to this action, stored with the commitment, or emitted in an event. Real bidders must generate a cryptographically random 32-byte secret locally and retain it until reveal.

At or after the commit deadline, anyone can call `start_reveal`. `reveal_bid` then accepts the authenticated participant's positive integer bid and original secret only before the reveal deadline. The bid must be no greater than that participant's request maximum and the round's available capital snapshot. The contract recomputes the Step 1 commitment; mismatches leave state unchanged. A successful reveal stores the bid amount but never the secret, and duplicate reveals fail. `get_commitment` returns a hash or `None`; `get_reveal` returns an accepted public bid or `None`.

At or after the reveal deadline, anyone can call `finalize_round`. It considers only accepted reveals. The smallest valid bid wins; equal bids are resolved by ascending wallet StrKey string. No valid reveal results in `Finalized` with no winner or winning bid. Finalization is one-time. The `Commit → Reveal → Finalized` transitions and deadlines are recorded on-chain, and all round participant lists are capped at 10 by Step 2.

Commitments and accepted reveals use persistent entries with TTL extension on write. The round's `valid_reveal_count`, phase, winner, and winning bid are stored in the persistent round record. A commit-phase round exposes neither a bid amount nor a secret through its reads. A hash can only hide a low-entropy bid if its secret is unpredictable; the frontend must use a secure random source and never log or publish the secret before reveal.

The winning wallet calls `settle(round_id, winner, due_date)` after finalization, with a future ledger timestamp for `due_date`. The contract checks both its available-pool accounting and actual token balance, transfers exactly the winning bid to the winner, marks that request `Funded`, and records the outstanding amount. Settlement can happen only once. No-winner rounds cannot settle.

The borrower calls `repay(round_id, member, amount)` with a positive amount no greater than the remaining debt. Each repayment transfers tokens back into the pool and updates both token and internal balances. Partial repayment reduces outstanding debt; full repayment marks the request `Repaid` and increments completed financing and repayment counters once. Missed-payment and default counters remain unchanged because the PRD does not define a schedule or overdue transition. Contract amounts are integer token base units; the frontend must convert using the configured asset's decimals.
